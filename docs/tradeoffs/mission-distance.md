# Mission Flight Time and Distance

To determine the farthest a drone can fly on a 30-minute mission with a roundtrip requirement, we use the basic formula for distance: 

$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time}$.

For a roundtrip, the drone travels a distance  to the destination and the same distance  back. Therefore, the total distance covered is , and the time available for a one-way leg is half of the total flight time (15 minutes).

### 1. Typical Speed Assumptions

"Typical" speeds vary by drone category. For consumer and prosumer models (like the DJI Mavic or Air series), the following ranges are common:

* **Cruising/Normal Speed:**  $30\text{--}35\text{ mph}$ ($48\text{--}56\text{ km/h}$)
* **Sport/High Speed:**  $40\text{--}45\text{ mph}$ ($64\text{--}72\text{ km/h}$)

### 2. Theoretical Maximum Distance

Using a "middle-of-the-road" typical speed of $40\text{ mph}$ ($64\text{ km/h}$):

* Total Flight Time: $30\text{ minutes}$ ($0.5\text{ hours}$)
* Total Distance Capacity: $40\text{ mph} \times 0.5\text{ hours} = 20\text{ miles}$ ($32\text{ km}$)
* Farthest One-Way Point:  $\mathbf{10\text{ miles}}$ ($\mathbf{16\text{ km}}$)

### 3. Realistic Distance (Accounting for Safety)

In real-world conditions, pilots never fly a battery to 0%. A standard safety margin is to land with **20% battery remaining** (using only 24 minutes of flight time). Additionally, flying at maximum speed consumes battery faster than the "optimum" cruising speed used for flight time ratings.

| Speed | Theoretical Max (One-Way) | Realistic Max (80% Battery) |
| ------------ | --- | --- |
| $30\text{ mph}$ ($48\text{ km/h}$) | $7.5\text{ miles}$ ($12\text{ km}$) | $6.0\text{ miles}$ ($9.6\text{ km}$) |
| $40\text{ mph}$ ($64\text{ km/h}$) | $10.0\text{ miles}$ ($16\text{ km}$) | $8.0\text{ miles}$ ($12.8\text{ km}$) |
| $45\text{ mph}$ ($72\text{ km/h}$) | $11.25\text{ miles}$ ($18\text{ km}$) | $9.0\text{ miles}$ ($14.4\text{ km}$) |

### Critical Factors to Consider

* **Wind:** A headwind on the way back can significantly reduce your range. If there is a  wind, your ground speed on the return leg might drop from  to , meaning you would need to turn back much sooner than the 15-minute mark.
* **Battery Drain vs. Speed:** Drones are most efficient at a specific "optimum cruise speed." Flying in "Sport Mode" to achieve $45\text{ mph}$  usually reduces the total flight time from 30 minutes to perhaps 20-22 minutes.
* **Signal Range:** While the battery might last for 10 miles, most consumer drone radio systems (like DJI OcuSync) have a maximum effective range of $6\text{--}9\text{ miles}$ ($10\text{--}15\text{ km}$) in ideal conditions, and often much less in urban areas with interference.

**Summary:** For a drone with a 30-minute battery, the farthest you should realistically plan to fly is about **7 to 9 miles (11 to 14 km)** away from your takeoff point to ensure a safe return.
